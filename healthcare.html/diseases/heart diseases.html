Heart Diseases: Categories, Symptoms & Age of Onset

Because "heart disease" is an umbrella term encompassing dozens of specific structural, electrical, vascular, and infectious conditions, medical organizations categorize them into major clinical groups.

Major Categories of Heart Disease

Heart Disease Category Specific Conditions Included Common Symptoms Typical Age of Onset
1. Coronary Artery Disease (CAD)
(Plaque buildup narrowing the heart's arteries)
  • Stable Angina
  • Unstable Angina
  • Myocardial Infarction (Heart Attack)
  • Silent Ischemia
  • Chest pain, pressure, tightness, or squeezing (angina)
  • Pain radiating to neck, jaw, back, stomach, or arms
  • Shortness of breath
  • Cold sweats, nausea, lightheadedness
  • Unusual fatigue (especially in women)
 45+ in men; 55+ in women
(Average first heart attack is ~65 in men / ~72 in women globally, though onset can occur at 45–50 in high-risk populations like South Asia)
2. Heart Arrhythmias
(Electrical system malfunctions causing irregular heartbeats)
  • Atrial Fibrillation (AFib)
  • Supraventricular Tachycardia (SVT)
  • Ventricular Tachycardia / Fibrillation
  • Bradycardia (slow heart rate)
  • Long QT Syndrome
  • Fluttering, racing, or pounding in the chest (palpitations)
  • Slow or skipped heartbeats
  • Dizziness or lightheadedness
  • Fainting (syncope) or near-fainting
  • Shortness of breath and chest discomfort
 Any age, depending on type:
• Inherited/SVT: Childhood to 30s
• AFib & degenerative arrhythmias: 60+ years
3. Heart Failure
(Heart muscle becomes too weak or stiff to pump blood efficiently)
  • Systolic Heart Failure (HFrEF)
  • Diastolic Heart Failure (HFpEF)
  • Left-sided vs. Right-sided Heart Failure
  • Congestive Heart Failure (CHF)
  • Shortness of breath during activity or when lying flat
  • Waking up breathless at night
  • Swelling (edema) in feet, ankles, legs, or abdomen
  • Persistent cough with white or pink mucus
  • Rapid weight gain (fluid retention) and severe fatigue
 65+ years
(Incidence doubles every decade after age 65)
4. Heart Valve Disease
(Valves narrow [stenosis], leak [regurgitation], or fail to close properly [prolapse])
  • Aortic Stenosis
  • Mitral Valve Prolapse (MVP)
  • Mitral Regurgitation
  • Tricuspid / Pulmonary Valve Disease
  • Rheumatic Heart Disease
  • Heart murmur (heard via stethoscope)
  • Shortness of breath, especially with exertion
  • Swollen ankles and feet
  • Fatigue and weakness
  • Dizziness or fainting
  • Irregular heartbeat
 • Congenital/MVP: Teens to 30s
• Rheumatic valve disease: 20s to 40s
• Calcific/degenerative (e.g., Aortic Stenosis): 65–75+ years
5. Cardiomyopathy
(Diseases of the heart muscle itself—thickening, stiffening, or enlarging)
  • Dilated Cardiomyopathy
  • Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM)
  • Restrictive Cardiomyopathy
  • Arrhythmogenic Right Ventricular Dysplasia (ARVD)
  • Takotsubo ("Broken Heart") Syndrome
  • Breathlessness at rest or with minimal exertion
  • Swelling in legs, ankles, and feet
  • Bloating of the abdomen due to fluid
  • Fainting during exercise (classic in HCM)
  • Chest pressure and rapid, fluttering heartbeats
 • Hypertrophic (genetic): Teens to 30s
• Dilated / Restrictive: 20 to 60 years
6. Congenital Heart Defects
(Structural abnormalities present at birth)
  • Atrial / Ventricular Septal Defects ("hole in the heart")
  • Tetralogy of Fallot
  • Coarctation of the Aorta
  • Patent Ductus Arteriosus (PDA)
  • Hypoplastic Left Heart Syndrome
  • In infants: Pale gray or blue skin/lips (cyanosis), rapid breathing, poor feeding, slow weight gain, swelling around eyes/belly
  • In older children/adults: Tiring easily during exercise, shortness of breath, swelling in hands/feet
 Birth to early childhood
(Milder defects may go undetected until adulthood)
7. Carditis (Heart Infections & Inflammation)
(Inflammation caused by viruses, bacteria, fungi, or autoimmune conditions)
  • Pericarditis: Inflammation of the outer sac
  • Myocarditis: Inflammation of the heart muscle
  • Endocarditis: Infection of the inner lining/valves
  • Sharp, stabbing chest pain that improves when sitting up and leaning forward (pericarditis)
  • Fever, chills, and night sweats (endocarditis)
  • Flu-like body aches and severe fatigue
  • Shortness of breath and rapid heartbeat
  • New heart murmur or blood spots under fingernails
 Any age
• Myocarditis/Pericarditis: Most common in young adults (20s–40s)
• Endocarditis: Peaks in older adults (60+) or those with prosthetic valves/IV drug use
8. Vascular & Aortic Heart Conditions
(Diseases affecting the major blood vessels connected to the heart)
  • Thoracic / Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm
  • Aortic Dissection
  • Pulmonary Hypertension
  • Sudden, severe "tearing" or "ripping" pain in the chest or upper back (aortic dissection)
  • Hoarseness, difficulty swallowing, or coughing
  • Shortness of breath and blue lips (pulmonary hypertension)
 55–75+ years
(Earlier in genetic connective tissue disorders like Marfan syndrome)

Specific & Rare Heart Conditions

In clinical cardiology (such as the ICD-11), there are over 60 distinct, named heart conditions when separating rare genetic disorders, specific congenital defects, and electrical syndromes.

1. Rare Electrical & Genetic Syndromes (Channelopathies)

  • Brugada Syndrome: Causes dangerous irregular beats in the lower chambers; often triggered during sleep or fever (more common in men of Asian descent, typically ages 30–40).
  • Wolff-Parkinson-White (WPW) Syndrome: An extra electrical pathway between the upper and lower chambers causing rapid heartbeats (teens to 20s).
  • Catecholaminergic Polymorphic Ventricular Tachycardia (CPVT): Triggered by adrenaline/exercise in children and young adults (ages 7–20).
  • Sick Sinus Syndrome: The heart's natural pacemaker fails to fire properly, causing alternating slow and fast heart rates (ages 65+).

2. Rare Structural & Tumor Conditions

  • Cardiac Tumors (e.g., Atrial Myxoma): Non-cancerous or cancerous growths inside the heart chambers that can block blood flow or mimic valve disease (ages 30–60).
  • Constrictive Pericarditis: Scarring and stiffening of the sac around the heart (often post-tuberculosis, radiation, or surgery) that prevents the heart from expanding (any age).
  • Cardiac Amyloidosis ("Stiff Heart Syndrome"): Abnormal protein deposits build up in the heart muscle, making it rigid (ages 60+).
  • Cardiac Sarcoidosis: Clusters of inflammatory cells (granulomas) form in the heart tissue, disrupting rhythm and pumping (ages 25–50).
  • Non-Compaction Cardiomyopathy (LVNC): A rare congenital condition where the lower left chamber muscle remains spongy instead of smooth and firm (infancy to adulthood).

3. Specialized Coronary & Microvascular Conditions

  • Coronary Microvascular Disease (Cardiac Syndrome X): Damage to the tiny microscopic blood vessels of the heart rather than the main arteries; causes chest pain despite "clear" angiograms (most common in women ages 45–60).
  • Prinzmetal's (Variant) Angina: Sudden, temporary spasms in a coronary artery that restrict blood flow, often occurring at rest or at night (ages 40–55).
  • Spontaneous Coronary Artery Dissection (SCAD): A sudden tear in the wall of a heart artery without traditional plaque buildup; affects mostly healthy women ages 30–50 (including postpartum).
  • Kawasaki Disease: Acute inflammation of blood vessels that can cause coronary artery aneurysms in children under 5.

4. Specific Complex Congenital Defects

  • Transposition of the Great Arteries (TGA): The two main arteries leaving the heart are switched (at birth).
  • Ebstein’s Anomaly: Malformed tricuspid valve sitting too low in the right ventricle (birth to adulthood).
  • Truncus Arteriosus: A single large blood vessel comes out of the heart instead of two separate ones (at birth).
  • Eisenmenger Syndrome: Long-term untreated congenital heart defects causing irreversible high blood pressure in the lungs (teens to 30s).
