Common illnesses span acute infections that occur across the lifespan and chronic conditions whose onset clusters around specific developmental or aging milestones.

Acute & Infectious Illnesses

Illness Typical Age of Onset/Peak Primary Symptoms Clinical References
Upper Respiratory Tract Infection (Common Cold) Any age; peaks in early childhood (6–12 episodes/year) Runny or stuffy nose, sneezing, mild sore throat, cough, low-grade fever CDC; NHS Inform
Influenza (Flu) Any age; higher complication rates under age 5 and over 65 Sudden high fever (38.8°C–40°C / 102°F–104°F), chills, severe body aches, dry cough, profound fatigue WHO; CDC
Acute Otitis Media (Middle Ear Infection) 6 months to 2 years (most common in early childhood) Ear pain (tugging at ears in infants), fussiness, trouble sleeping, fever, fluid drainage, muffled hearing AAP (American Academy of Pediatrics)
Streptococcal Pharyngitis (Strep Throat) 5 to 15 years Sudden severe sore throat, pain when swallowing, fever over 38.3°C (101°F), red/swollen tonsils with white patches, swollen neck lymph nodes (typically no cough) CDC; IDSA
Hand, Foot, and Mouth Disease Under 5 years Fever, sore throat, painful mouth sores, non-itchy red spots or blisters on palms and soles CDC; Raising Children Network

Chronic & Non-Communicable Illnesses

Chronic conditions follow more predictable epidemiological patterns driven by genetics, hormonal shifts, and cumulative metabolic or mechanical wear.

Illness Typical Age of Onset Primary Symptoms Clinical References
Asthma Childhood (often before age 5); secondary adult-onset peak Episodic wheezing, shortness of breath, chest tightness, persistent cough (often worse at night or with exercise) GINA (Global Initiative for Asthma); WHO
Allergic Rhinitis (Hay Fever) Childhood to early adulthood (typically before age 20) Sneezing, clear runny nose, nasal congestion, itchy/watery eyes, itchy throat AAAAI; NHS Inform
Type 1 Diabetes Bimodal peak: ages 4–7 and 10–14 (puberty) Excessive thirst, frequent urination, unexplained weight loss, extreme fatigue, blurred vision ADA (American Diabetes Association); MoHFW India
Major Depressive Disorder Late adolescence to mid-20s (median onset ~25 years) Persistent low mood, loss of interest/pleasure, sleep and appetite changes, fatigue, difficulty concentrating NIMH; DSM-5-TR
Hypertension (High Blood Pressure) Mid-adulthood (typically 40–50 years) Usually asymptomatic ("silent"); severe elevations may cause headaches, shortness of breath, or nosebleeds WHO; AHA
Type 2 Diabetes Middle adulthood (45+ years globally; ~35–45 years in South Asian populations) Gradual onset of increased thirst, frequent urination, fatigue, slow-healing sores, numbness/tingling in extremities WHO; IDF; MoHFW India
Osteoarthritis 50+ years (risk rises sharply after age 45) Joint pain that worsens with activity, brief morning stiffness (less than 30 minutes), reduced range of motion, joint swelling ACR (American College of Rheumatology); CDC
Coronary Artery Disease 55+ years in men; 65+ years in women Chest pain or pressure (angina), shortness of breath on exertion, fatigue; may present acutely as a heart attack AHA; ESC
