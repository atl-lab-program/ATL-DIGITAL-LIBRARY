Kidney Diseases: Categories, Symptoms & Age of Onset

Kidney disease occurs when the kidneys are damaged and cannot filter waste, balance fluids and electrolytes, or regulate blood pressure and red blood cell production as effectively as they should. It can develop suddenly (acute kidney injury, or AKI) or progress gradually over months to years (chronic kidney disease, or CKD). Because early-stage kidney disease often causes no obvious symptoms, screening with a blood estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) test and a urine albumin-creatinine ratio (uACR) test is key for early detection.

Symptoms of Kidney Disease

Early stages of CKD frequently present with no noticeable signs. As kidney function declines and wastes, fluid, or electrolytes accumulate—or as anemia and mineral imbalances develop—symptoms become more apparent:

• Fluid & Urine Changes: Swelling (edema) around the eyes, hands, ankles, or feet; foamy or bubbly urine (a sign of protein leaking into urine); blood in the urine; or changes in urination frequency (such as waking more often to urinate or producing less urine in advanced failure).

• Energy & Thinking: Persistent fatigue, weakness, feeling cold, dizziness, trouble concentrating (" brain fog ") , or sleep disturbances.

• Digestive & Skin Signs: Loss of appetite, nausea, vomiting, or dry, persistent itching.

• Cardiopulmonary & Blood Pressure Signs: Hard-to-control high blood pressure or shortness of breath (which can result from fluid buildup in the lungs or anemia).

When to seek promptmedical care:: Sudden shortness of breath, chest pain or pressure, severe swelling, inability to urinate, or confusion/extreme lethargy require urgent medical evaluation, as complications like fluid overload or high blood potassium (hyperkalemia) can be serious.

Category Key Causes & Risk Factors How They Affect The Kidneys

    Leading Chronic Conditions

    Type 1 or type 2 diabetes,high blood pressure(hypertension),

High blood sugar and elevated blood pressure injure delicate blood vessels and filtering units over time.
    Inflamation & Autoimmune

    Glomerular diseases (such as IgA nephropathy), lupus, vasculitis/autoimmune disorders

    Immune activity or inflammation damages the kidney's filtering structures.
    Structural & Genetic

    Polycystic kidney disease (PKD), inherited kidney disorders, urinary tract blockages (e.g., kidney stones, enlarged prostate), vesicoureteral reflux

    Cysts, backed-up urine, or obstructed outflow increase pressure and impair tissue function.
    Acute Triggers & Medications

    Severe dehydration, infections/sepsis, heart or liver conditions, frequent NSAIDs or other kidney-toxic drugs

    Can trigger sudden acute kidney injury or worsen underlying chronic damage.
    Broader Risk Factors

    Family history of kidney disease, older age, heart disease, obesity, smoking

    Raise the likelihood of developing or accelerating kidney decline.
